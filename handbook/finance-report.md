# Finance Report Guide

The finance report gives your vestry or church council a two-page picture of
the church's money each month, in your church's brand. It reads your books in
QuickBooks and never changes them.

## Getting started: finance onboarding

Say "Set up our finances." Onboarding runs once, in four stages, and you can
stop and pick up again at any point.

1. **Look.** The agent reads your books and writes a plain-language overview
   for you, the pastor, with questions for your treasurer. The board does not
   see this overview.
2. **First report.** The agent drafts the report's settings from your books
   (which accounts are everyday cash, how income and spending group into a
   few plain categories, your budget) and asks you to confirm them. Then it
   builds a sample report.
3. **Make the books report-ready.** A short checklist, reviewed once with your
   treasurer: are online payments recorded in full, are bank accounts
   reconciled, are months closed, is restricted money identified? Your
   treasurer or bookkeeper makes any changes in their own books. An item you
   cannot fix yet can be waived with a reason; the report keeps saying so.
4. **Lock the monthly report.** Agree when the books close each month and
   when the report is ready, and who approves it. The treasurer approves the
   first real report, and the monthly routine begins.

You need QuickBooks Online connected to your AI app, and a QuickBooks login
for your church's company (your treasurer or bookkeeper usually has one).

## Each month

Say "Build this month's finance report." The report answers the same
questions every month, so board members learn to read it:

- Can we pay our bills?
- Are we on plan, compared with the budget and with last year?
- Where does our money come from, and where does it go?
- What is on the watch list, and did anything change?
- Is our cash growing or shrinking over the years?

Page 1 shows this month. Page 2 carries the watch list, the obligations the
board oversees (payroll filings, pension, assessment, insurance), and the
longer view. A church whose books are less than about eighteen months old
gets page 1 and the watch list only until the history exists.

## What the report promises

- Every figure comes from your books, with the date it was read. Totals are
  checked against QuickBooks before anything is drawn.
- Anything the books cannot yet show is stated as a caveat, in plain words.
- If earlier months change after a report went out, the next report says so.
- A dollar figure in the written summary must match the books, or the
  report cannot be marked final.
- The report is a draft until your treasurer approves it.

The report describes your books. It is not financial advice, and it does not
do bookkeeping.
