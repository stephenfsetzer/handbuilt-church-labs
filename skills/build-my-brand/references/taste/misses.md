# The misses log

Every time a pastor overrules work the critic passed, the miss goes here: what
happened, why it was wrong, and what it became. This is how the art director
gets better without retraining a model: its judgment improves because this
log does.

Write entries in general terms. Never name a church, a person or a place, and
never paste a church's words.

An entry starts as a **note**: the critic weighs it. It becomes a **rule**
(a "never") only when the same miss happens again, or when the person who
owns the product confirms it. One reaction is taste, not yet a rule.

## Rules

| Miss | Rule |
|---|---|
| Documentary photos of people printed in a strong brand color made them look lurid | Photos of people keep their true color in the house grade, or go plain black and white. Never a brand-color print or duotone over people |
| Every photo was tinted the same dark brand color | One treatment per set of photos, chosen by the set; a warm, natural grade by default |
| A drawing of part of the building didn't match the real building | Never draw a real part of the building unless it follows a real photo, and name the photo |
| Titles were clipped by or bled off the edge | No text within 24 px of an edge. Break the grid with shapes, never by cropping words |
| The mark was redrawn or assembled from its parts | The mark comes only from the official files, never redrawn |
| The signature color sat as one of three equal blocks with white and a dark blue | The flag test: the signature color never sits as one of three equal blocks with paper and a dark blue |
| The lockup's second line was a few pixels tall in the website header | Every lockup is checked in the website header at 30 to 44 px tall before the brand is approved |
| The address was repeated in several sections and had to be pulled out page by page | Facts live once in site data. On a page, a fact appears once |
| The site leaned on effects (a typing intro, a clock) instead of pictures and type | Effects earn their place in the motion stage: two or three signature moments at most |
| Pages were built one at a time without a plan, and the whole didn't add up | A site map, the words and a page score come before design |
| The palette grew from five colors to nine, each added to fix a flat piece | A flat piece is fixed with scale, type or a better image, never a new brand color. Photo colors belong to a campaign |
| A design kept copying the last good section | Every section changes at least one of lead, ground, scale or layout from the one before |
| A look nobody picked was added to the kit | A new look goes to the pastor as an option, never into the kit |

## Notes

| Miss | Note |
|---|---|
| Photos already used on one page reappeared on another | Check the photo ledger before assigning a photo; near-duplicates count as used |
| Three thin columns of photos made the church look like little happens there | Show abundance with a full mosaic or one large image |
| A hero followed every rule and passed, but was well below the other approved heroes: a template half-and-half split, the mark shown twice with one copy as a big slab, and no headline idea | The critic judges quality against an approved piece of the same kind first, and redirects work with no single idea however well it follows the rules |
| Campaign looks were tidy and followed the rules, but four of six leaned on the same arch, one was only an effect, and none had a line of the church's voice | Each look borrows a real kind of printed object and carries one line of voice and one true fact; no device carries two looks |
| Inside pages passed the score rules but dropped the hero's idea, left a large shape empty, and two churches' pages shared one skeleton | The hero's device carries through holding content; pages are judged filled to their word budgets and beside the last church's same page |
