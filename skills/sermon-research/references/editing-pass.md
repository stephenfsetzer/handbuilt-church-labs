# Research editing pass

Use a fresh context when available. Read the draft against the writer's cards, source
locators, the active template, and these methodology sections: Required
shape, Writing standard, and Quality read before recording. Do not read the
rest of the methodology in this pass. Your job includes substantive
revision and a justified acceptance decision.

First assess the thought. Can you explain the actual interpretive choice, its
textual grounds, and what changes with each reading? Has accessible material
crowded out substantial research? Does the cultural selection deserve attention
now? Does any human-sciences entry rest on an opened primary study or review
and state what it cannot tell a preacher? If the evidence is too thin, return a specific research gap. Polished prose
cannot repair missing scholarship.

Strike process language: any sentence about what was searched, opened,
unavailable, or watched, and any reference to the brief, the dossier, or the
researcher. Rewrite the limit as attribution and cited scope. The runtime
rejects common process phrases, so a leak blocks recording.

Check the closing questions. Each must be specific to this passage and this
brief, and together they should draw out where the pastor leans and what that
choice costs. Replace any question that could sit under another passage.

Then assess the writing. Does the opening make a real difficulty worth pursuing?
Does each paragraph develop the previous thought? Reorganize author summaries,
remove duplicated background, shorten explanations that do no work, and revise
stiff phrasing into natural speech. Preserve the author's meaning and genuine
uncertainty. Moving names around or adding contractions alone is insufficient.

Reopen consequential source passages yourself, especially quotations, contested
readings, cultural scenes, and claims changed in editing: at least three, and
every direct quotation you keep. Decide the full list first and issue those
fetches together in one turn; sequential rechecks are the slowest part of a
run. A lead's or parent's earlier source review
does not discharge this check; your recheck is the independent one. If a
source cannot be reopened, say so in the record and lower the claim's
confidence. Check that attribution, scope, and confidence survive the
revision. Removing an author's name does not cure
an unsupported claim. A new factual or interpretive attribution requires source
support. Keep traditional readings intellectually serious and give credible
alternatives a fair account without inventing consensus or conflict.

Edit the draft in place with targeted edits. Do not regenerate the brief
from scratch unless its structure fails; a full rewrite doubles the run's
cost and time without improving the evidence. Return the edited brief and
an editorial record of at most 150 words:

- accepted, or needs research/writing revision, with the concrete reason;
- material revisions and why they improve the reader's understanding;
- consequential claims rechecked, each with the locator you reopened and what
  you confirmed there; an entry that cites only the cards or a prior review
  does not count;
- remaining limitations and whether they affect use of the brief.

Acceptance requires substantive research, coherent inquiry, natural prose,
source fidelity, and meaningful cultural selection when enabled. Formatting and
word count are separate mechanical checks. The lead reviews the evidence and
final artifact before recording. If a bounded repair still fails, report that
outcome honestly rather than cycling through invisible revisions.
