# Research Source Directory

These are places to look. They are not a weekly checklist. The pastor's
tradition, `sermon.research_preferences`, available licensed resources, and the
needs of the passage determine which sources matter.

## Lectionary and biblical text

Confirm readings from two retrieved hosts appropriate to the church:

- Revised Common Lectionary: [Vanderbilt Divinity Library](https://lectionary.library.vanderbilt.edu/)
- Episcopal and Anglican: [The Lectionary Page](https://lectionarypage.net/)
- Anglican Church of Canada: [lectionary.anglican.ca](https://lectionary.anglican.ca/)
- Roman Catholic: [USCCB Daily Readings](https://bible.usccb.org/readings)
- Lutheran and RCL: [Working Preacher](https://www.workingpreacher.org/)
- Methodist: [UMC Discipleship Ministries](https://www.umcdiscipleship.org/)

For text comparison and language orientation, use the church's licensed or
preferred translation plus tools such as [Bible Gateway](https://www.biblegateway.com/),
[Blue Letter Bible](https://www.blueletterbible.org/), and
[STEP Bible](https://www.stepbible.org/).

A non-lectionary preacher confirms the selected text directly with the pastor,
opens one published text reference in the configured translation, and records
both facts rather than inventing a calendar authority. A scheduled run may
research that confirmed passage later, but it may not choose the passage.
The published Bible source verifies the citation and translation. It does not
prove that the pastor selected the passage; only the pastor's date-bound
confirmation does.

## Primary theological and historical texts

- [New Advent Fathers](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/)
- [Christian Classics Ethereal Library](https://ccel.org/)
- [Early Christian Commentary](https://www.earlychristiancommentary.com/)
- [Perseus](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/)
- [Penelope at the University of Chicago](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/)

Open the primary text when available. Secondary attributions to ancient
writers are often copied with the wrong work, section, or wording.

## Commentary and scholarship

- [Working Preacher](https://www.workingpreacher.org/)
- [Enter the Bible](https://enterthebible.org/)
- [Lectionary Greek](https://lectionarygreek.blogspot.com/)
- [Theology of Work](https://www.theologyofwork.org/)

Use major critical commentary series through the pastor's library when
available. Read across traditions, especially when a vivid interpretation has
become familiar. The strongest counterargument often reveals what that reading
costs.

## Language and background

- [Bill Mounce Greek Dictionary](https://www.billmounce.com/greek-dictionary)
- [Blue Letter Bible interlinear](https://www.blueletterbible.org/)
- BDAG, HALOT, TDNT, and comparable references when licensed
- [Biblical Archaeology Society](https://www.biblicalarchaeology.org/)
- University, museum, and primary historical collections

Popular word studies and local-color claims are frequently contested. Find a
credible objection before letting one carry the sermon.

## Human sciences

- [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/)
- [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/)
- [APA PsycNet](https://psycnet.apa.org/)
- [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/)
- [Nature](https://www.nature.com/)

Cite the study when it is reachable. Judge scholarship by whether later work
has superseded it. A fixed news-age rule does not apply to every field.

## Contemporary context and the arts

Use responsible current reporting, official data, and relevant institutional
sources. The research brief does not research or characterize the pastor's
actual parish, neighborhood, or city. Local reporting belongs only in the
pastor's subsequent work after the pastor supplies the local connection.
Check dates closely when describing a current policy, condition, leader, or
program.

Literature, theater, film, music, and visual art belong only when a citable
source or the pastor's real fluency deepens the text. Proximity and thematic
resemblance do not create an interpretive connection.

## Source hygiene

1. Retrieve the page or text.
2. Prefer primary sources for quotations and historical claims.
3. Record publication and retrieval dates.
4. Find the counterargument to a vivid detail.
5. Name important material that was unavailable.
6. Use the source ledger and cite support where the claim appears.
