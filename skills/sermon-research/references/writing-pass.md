# Research writing pass

You wrote the cards; now read the active template (the church's own
`sermons/research-brief-template.md` when it exists, otherwise the shipped
one; take its title line and headings from that file, and name which file
you used in the run notes) and these methodology
sections: Required shape, Depth and length, Writing standard, and Questions
for reflection. Write the brief directly from your cards and the sources you
opened. You may reopen a source to check wording. Your output is a research brief for pastoral discernment. It is not
a sermon manuscript and does not choose the sermon center.

Find the intellectual movement before drafting: the difficulty in the passage,
what a substantial reading helps us see, and what another argument develops or
complicates. Let the text's concrete details carry the reader into that inquiry.
Use the required outer headings, with passage-specific questions inside them.
Give each question one home and let paragraphs develop it instead of repeating
background or introducing another author on a schedule.

Treat sources as people making reasoned claims. Explain their grounds and
preserve their strongest account before testing it. Differences in emphasis
can be fruitful without becoming disagreements. Give the reader enough of the
reasoning to make a judgment. Do not assign every source the same generic
pastoral warning. Keep your synthesis distinguishable from sourced findings.

Write as a thoughtful colleague: direct language, natural contractions,
concrete subjects, and rhythm fitted to the thought. A paragraph should make
the next one necessary or interesting. Vividness comes from the passage and
evidence, never invented scenes, quotations, or local knowledge. Source access mechanics belong in notes, never in the brief; a limit that
matters is carried by the attribution and the cited portion. The brief is
finished copy and never refers to itself, the dossier, or the researcher.

Use the cards' supported claims and cite them at the point of use. Reopen a
source when necessary; do not infer missing support from its title or author.
If the argument needs a fact the cards do not establish, go back to the
sources and open it. Do not fill it from memory or cover it with a vague
citation.
A work listed for further reading cannot substantiate an unread argument.

Select cultural material, and a human-sciences finding when that stream is
enabled, for the question it helps the pastor think through. Use the two
Contemporary Convergence subheadings the methodology names.
Give a concrete reason it merits attention now and show where the comparison
stops. Avoid a paragraph that merely announces a thematic resemblance.

Save the draft before editing begins. Hand the editor the draft, your cards
with locators, and any unresolved questions. Do not mark your own prose as
independently approved.

## Preflight before handing off

Draft once. Fix a checklist item by editing the draft in place, never by
writing a second full draft. Run these checks yourself; each has blocked a
real record attempt:

- Header values match the recorded readings exactly; the Text line carries
  the citation alone, with no translation suffix.
- Every entry in References is a markdown link, and every ledger URL
  appears there.
- No em or en dash; no process phrase; no "your congregation" wording.
- Interpretive Conversations has two to four question headings; Questions
  for reflection is the last heading and every line ends with a question
  mark.
- Body length is 1,600 to 2,200 words before References.
