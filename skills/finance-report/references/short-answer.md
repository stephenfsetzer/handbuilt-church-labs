# The short answer

Two or three sentences at the top of page 1. It is the only judgment on the
page; everything else is computed. The builder drafts it; the agent refines
it; the treasurer approves or edits it before the report is final.

## Rules

1. **Start with the month.** What was recorded, in one clause: "August:
   $1,208 recorded income and $14,959 recorded spending, including $4,654
   for legal fees." A month with no income entered is not a bad month; the
   bookkeeper has not posted it yet. Then the first sentence says so and the
   year is read through the month before: "September is not yet recorded: no
   income has been entered and only some bills, so read the year through
   August. The year through August is $30,000 short, ..."
2. **Then the year, against last year.** "That leaves us $22,725 short for
   the year, still better than this point last year ($27,209 short), and
   last year ended $43,308 ahead." Compare with the same point last year,
   not only with the budget, because church income is uneven through the
   year.
3. **End with the bills.** "We can pay our bills: about two and a half
   months of spending is in the bank." Under one and a half months, say cash
   is tight.
4. Name a cause only if the data shows it (a named payment, a named bill).
   Never guess at reasons.
5. Plain words, whole dollars, no accounting terms, no em dashes or en
   dashes. No more than 70 words.
6. Nothing that belongs to the treasurer first (restricted funds possibly
   spent, filings, arrears).
